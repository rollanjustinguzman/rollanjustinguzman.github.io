Zen Marine Charter · Destin, Florida

Selling the last weekends of the season.

A luxury yacht charter running a September discount against a hard deadline. The brief was to make a price promotion feel like an invitation rather than a clearance sale, so the offer sits quietly underneath the image instead of shouting over it.

Why it works

Charter buyers decide fast and compare on feel, not features. So the set leads with water and light, keeps the discount to one line, and puts every practical question — how many people, how long, what's included, what it costs — on a single card that can be sent straight into a DM.

Motion

Stills from short-form edits.

Short-form lives or dies in the first second, so I cut for the hook first and build the rest of the edit backwards from it. Captions are set by hand rather than auto-generated, because the timing of a word appearing is part of the rhythm.

Everything is delivered in the aspect ratio and safe margins of the platform it is going to, with a horizontal variant when the same footage has to work as an ad.

Systems

What sits behind the content.

Content only compounds if a system is holding it. On the accounts I run, that means one board where every piece has an owner, a keyword target, and a state.

The same applies after the click. In GoHighLevel I set up the pipeline stages, the email and SMS sequences that fire against them, and the Appointwise agent that qualifies a lead and puts it on the calendar. In Dubsado and HoneyBook, the equivalent for contracts, invoices, and client onboarding.
